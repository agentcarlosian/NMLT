\author{Carlosian\\

\date{27 August 2026}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}\sloppy
Refinement theories often treat a concrete step as hidden when it maps to
equality of abstract state. Open systems, separately, compose by
synchronization on shared labels. The combination suggests a congruence
rule: if $C$ refines $A$, then $C$ in parallel with a peer $D$ refines
$A$ in parallel with $D$. We show that the rule is false for a one-step
refinement mapping with stuttering (not conventional weak simulation). In
Lean~4 we construct a sender whose hidden $\ping$ refines a step-free
abstraction in isolation, wire $\ping$ to a receiver that flips an observed
bit, and prove that no observation-preserving one-step refinement relates
the composites.
The diagnosis is local rather than exotic: abstract-state equality is not
contextual silence when the hidden label remains connected.

The same development yields a matching positive theorem. On the same
labelled-transition fragment, the one-step refinement \emph{does} lift
through parallel composition if three interface conditions hold: no hidden
label is connected, wirings are covered and reflected, and the label map
preserves independence of unconnected labels. For the \emph{default}
composite classifiers, each named premise is independently indispensable
in the hypothesis-independence sense---witnessed by
\lean{pingReceive_violates_noHiddenBoundary},
\lean{CollidedAbstractWire.isolation_necessary_for_default_lift}, and
\lean{ExtraAbstractWire.wiring_necessary_for_default_lift}.
We also record a sibling exact-action congruence theorem in a stronger
open-system model where internal actions cannot synchronize. We do not
claim that every interface condition is necessary in a stronger sense than
hypothesis-independence, nor resource, grade, or fairness congruence, nor
a verified compiler from source programs to these transition systems.
\end{abstract}

%------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Two classical ideas sit next to each other in almost every account of
open-system refinement. The first is \emph{hiding}: a concrete step need
not match an abstract step if it leaves the abstract state unchanged.
The second is \emph{synchronization}: two components may be required to
take a joint step on a pair of connected labels, and a connected label
may not move alone. Each idea is well understood. Together they invite
an apparently harmless congruence rule.

The precise candidate we study---matching the Lean package
\lean{weakConditionalCongruence_safety}---is not a single shared wiring.
Fix a peer $D$, concrete and abstract wirings $K_C$ and $K_A$, a local
witness $R : C\preceqhm_{h,m} A$, and the default composite classifiers
\lean{compositeHiddenOf}$(h)$ and \lean{compositeMapOf}$(m)$. The
tempting rule is
\begin{equation}
\frac{R : C \preceqhm_{h,m} A}
     {\begin{array}{@{}c@{}}
      C \parallel_{K_C} D \;\preceqhm_{h^\parallel,m^\parallel}\;
      A \parallel_{K_A} D
      \\[2pt]
      \scriptstyle h^\parallel=\mathtt{compositeHiddenOf}(h),\;
      m^\parallel=\mathtt{compositeMapOf}(m)
      \end{array}}
\tag{Candidate-Congruence}
\label{eq:candidate}
\end{equation}
A common special case takes $K_C=K_A=K$ and $m=\mathrm{id}$; that sketch
is already false, and the general form is what the positive theorem
repairs under interface premises. Writing $\preceqhm$ without subscripts
for the special case, the unconditional rule
\begin{equation}
\frac{C \preceqhm A}
     {C \parallel_K D \preceqhm A \parallel_K D}
\tag{Invalid-Congruence}
\label{eq:invalid}
\end{equation}
would be a lemma if hiding meant silence in every context. It is not.

This paper mechanizes a minimal counterexample and a matching repair.
A concrete sender $C$ has a single action $\ping$. Declared hidden,
$\ping$ does not change $C$'s state or observation, so $C$ one-step
refines a step-free abstract sender $A$. A receiver $D$ flips an
observed Boolean on $\receive$. Connect $\ping$ to $\receive$. The
composite $C\parallel_K D$ can take a synchronized step that changes
the pair's observation from $(\false,\false)$ to $(\false,\true)$.
The abstract composite $A\parallel_K D$ has no corresponding left
step with which to synchronize. Treating the product step as hidden
collapses the two composite states under the refinement map and forces
$\false=\true$ by observation preservation. Treating it as visible
leaves the abstract product stuck. Either way,
\eqref{eq:invalid} fails for this refinement and this composition
operator. (The visible-classification failure is immediate from $A$'s
empty step relation; it is not yet a separately named checked Lean
theorem.)

The failure is not an artifact of an eccentric hiding rule. The
candidate relation is a \emph{one-step refinement mapping with
stuttering}: hidden steps preserve the abstract state; visible steps
match exactly one abstract step; observations are preserved. It is
\emph{not} conventional weak simulation: there are no $\tau^*$-paths,
no multi-step abstract matching, and no relational simulation witness
beyond a state map. The composition
operator is the obvious synchronized product: linked labels must move
together, unlinked labels move independently. What the local rule does
not see is that a hidden label may still be \emph{connected}. Local
abstract-state equality then coexists with a peer observation change.

That diagnosis suggests a repair rather than an abandonment of hiding.
On the same labelled-transition fragment we prove a conditional
congruence theorem: if no hidden label is connected, if the concrete
and abstract wirings cover and reflect each other, and if the label
map sends unconnected concrete labels to unconnected abstract labels,
then left-component one-step refinement lifts through the product under
the default classifiers of \eqref{eq:candidate}. The three premises are
independently indispensable for that default lift. Dropping the first
recovers the ping/receive counterexample
(\lean{pingReceive_violates_noHiddenBoundary}). Dropping the third,
while keeping wiring coverage, admits a second counterexample in which
a non-injective label map collapses an independent concrete action onto
a connected abstract name
(\lean{CollidedAbstractWire.isolation_necessary_for_default_lift}).
Dropping wiring coverage while keeping the other two admits a third
(\lean{ExtraAbstractWire.wiring_necessary_for_default_lift}).

A sibling result lives in a different model. Exact-action (strong)
refinement of open systems with explicit ports, in which internal
actions are ineligible for synchronization by construction, does lift
through parallel composition under whole-wiring equivalence. We report
that theorem because it is checked and because it shows that an
interface-aware positive result is available once hiding through
connections is forbidden structurally. It is not a proof of the weak
one-step theorem, not residual RFC~0008 conditional congruence, and we
do not treat it as one.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A Lean~4 mechanization of \eqref{eq:invalid}'s failure: local
  sender refinement, an enabled synchronized step, and the absence of
  any observation-preserving one-step refinement of the composites
  (Theorems~\ref{thm:sender}--\ref{thm:no-composite}).
  \item The diagnosis that local abstract-state equality is not
  contextual silence at a live connection, and the corresponding
  interface obligation
  $\mathsf{I\textrm{-}NO\textrm{-}HIDDEN\textrm{-}BOUNDARY}$.
  \item A checked one-step observation-safety lift on the same small
  model (Theorem~\ref{thm:weak-safety}), with positive controls and
  hypothesis-independence lemmas for isolation reflection and wiring
  coverage (Lemmas~\ref{lem:isolation-necessary},%
  \ref{lem:wiring-necessary}).
  \item A restricted exact-action product congruence theorem in a
  stronger open-system model (Theorem~\ref{thm:strong-lift}),
  separated from the weak development and not residual C1.
\end{enumerate}

The host development is \nmlt{}, an Apache-2.0 research kernel for
behavior-indexed types and evidence-carrying composition. The theorems
of this paper concern the labelled-transition fragment in
\lean{NMLT.Core.Transition} and the modules cited in
Appendix~\ref{app:defs}. We do not claim a verified translation from
\nmlt{} source programs to these transition systems, nor congruence
for capabilities, grades, or fairness, nor that Theorem~\ref{thm:weak-safety}
closes full RFC~0008 conditional congruence.

\section{The model}
\label{sec:model}
%------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Notation follows the Lean development in \lean{NMLT.Core.Transition}.

\begin{definition}[LTS]
\label{def:lts}
For types $\mathit{Label}$ and $\mathit{Obs}$, a labelled transition
system is a record
\[
\langle
  \mathit{State},\;
  \mathit{init} : \mathit{State}\to\mathrm{Prop},\;
  \mathit{step} : \mathit{State}\to\mathit{Label}\to\mathit{State}\to\mathrm{Prop},\;
  \mathit{observe} : \mathit{State}\to\mathit{Obs}
\rangle
\]
(\lean{NMLT.LTS}).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[One-step refinement mapping with stuttering]
\label{def:weak}
Fix LTSs $C$ and $A$ with a common observation type, a hiding map
$h : \mathit{Label}_C\to\mathrm{Bool}$, and a label map
$m : \mathit{Label}_C\to\mathit{Label}_A$.
A witness of $C\preceqhm_{h,m} A$ (\lean{WeakRefines}) is a state map
$\mu$ such that:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{init:} $C.\mathit{init}\,s$ implies $A.\mathit{init}\,(\mu\,s)$;
  \item \textbf{observe:} $C.\mathit{observe}\,s = A.\mathit{observe}\,(\mu\,s)$;
  \item \textbf{hidden step (stutter):} if $C$ steps $s\xrightarrow{\ell}t$ and
  $h(\ell)=\true$, then $\mu\,s = \mu\,t$;
  \item \textbf{visible step:} if $C$ steps $s\xrightarrow{\ell}t$ and
  $h(\ell)=\false$, then $A$ steps $\mu\,s\xrightarrow{m(\ell)}\mu\,t$.
\end{itemize}
A hidden concrete step is required to preserve the \emph{abstract
state}, not merely the concrete observation. There is no multi-step
abstract matching: a visible concrete step corresponds to exactly one
abstract step. In particular, this is \emph{not} conventional weak
simulation in the process-calculus sense (no $\tau^*$-closure, no
relational witness relating states after silent sequences). It is closer
to a one-step Abadi--Lamport refinement mapping with stuttering on
hidden labels.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Synchronized parallel composition]
\label{def:par}
Product labels are $\mathsf{left}\,\ell$, $\mathsf{right}\,r$, or
$\sync(\ell,r)$ (\lean{ParallelLabel}).
A connection $K$ is a relation on left and right labels
(\lean{Connection.linked}).
The product $L\parallel_K R$ (\lean{parallel}) has state space
$L.\mathit{State}\times R.\mathit{State}$, componentwise
initialization and observation, and steps:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\mathsf{left}\,\ell$ only if $\ell$ is not linked to any right
  label, the left component steps, and the right state is unchanged;
  \item $\mathsf{right}\,r$ symmetrically;
  \item $\sync(\ell,r)$ only if $K$ links $\ell$ to $r$ and both
  components step.
\end{itemize}
Linked labels cannot move independently. Unlinked labels cannot
synchronize.
\end{definition}

This is a small model. It has no ports, directions, capabilities, or
fairness. That is deliberate: the counterexample and the one-step lift
should be checkable against the same four-field LTS, without an
intervening encoding.

\section{The counterexample}
\label{sec:counterexample}
%------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Let $\mathit{SenderLabel} ::= \ping$ and
$\mathit{ReceiverLabel} ::= \receive$.
The constructions are those of
\lean{NMLT.Counterexamples.CompositionCongruence}.

\begin{definition}[Abstract sender $A$]
\label{def:A}
\lean{abstractSender}: state $\mathbf{1}$, every state initial, no
steps, observation constantly $\false$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Concrete sender $C$]
\label{def:C}
\lean{concreteSender}: state $\mathbf{1}$, every state initial, a
step for label $\ping$ to any post-state, observation constantly
$\false$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Receiver $D$]
\label{def:D}
\lean{receiver}: state $\mathrm{Bool}$, only $\false$ initial; a step
holds iff the label is $\receive$, the pre-state is $\false$, and the
post-state is $\true$; observation is the identity on $\mathrm{Bool}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Connection and composites]
\label{def:KD}
\lean{connection} links $\ping$ to $\receive$.
Write $C\parallel_K D$ for \lean{concreteComposite} and
$A\parallel_K D$ for \lean{abstractComposite}.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Local refinement}

\begin{definition}[Sender hiding]
\label{def:senderHidden}
\lean{senderHidden} hides every sender label. The label map is
$\mathrm{id}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Standalone sender refinement]
\label{thm:sender}
The structure \lean{senderRefinement} inhabits
\begin{center}\footnotesize
\lean{WeakRefines}\ $C$\ $A$\ \lean{senderHidden}\ $\mathrm{id}$.
\end{center}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Take $\mu(\_)=()$. Initialization and observation are immediate.
Every concrete step uses $\ping$, which is hidden, and $\mu$ is
constant, so the hidden-step obligation is reflexivity. The
visible-step obligation is vacuous.
\end{proof}

Thus the premise of \eqref{eq:invalid} holds for this $C$ and $A$.

\subsection{The composite}

\begin{definition}[Composite hiding]
\label{def:compositeHidden}
\lean{compositeHidden} hides synchronized product labels and leaves
independent left and right labels visible:
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\mathtt{compositeHidden}(\sync(\_,\_))=\true,\\
&\mathtt{compositeHidden}(\mathsf{left}\_)=
 \mathtt{compositeHidden}(\mathsf{right}\_)=\false.
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Concrete synchronization]
\label{thm:sync}
\[
\begin{aligned}
&(C\parallel_K D).\mathit{step}\\
&\qquad ((),\false)\;
  \sync(\ping,\receive)\;
  ((),\true).
\end{aligned}
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$K$ links $\ping$ to $\receive$; $C$ may step on $\ping$; $D$ steps
$\false\to\true$ on $\receive$
(\texttt{concrete\-Synchronization}).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Endpoint observations]
\label{lem:obs}
$(C\parallel_K D).\mathit{observe}\,((),\false)=(\false,\false)$ and
$(C\parallel_K D).\mathit{observe}\,((),\true)=(\false,\true)$.
In particular the second components differ.
\end{lemma}

\begin{theorem}[No composite one-step refinement]
\label{thm:no-composite}
There is no inhabitant of
\begin{quote}
\raggedright\footnotesize
\lean{WeakRefines}
  $(C\parallel_K D)$
  $(A\parallel_K D)$
  \lean{compositeHidden}
  $\mathrm{id}$.
\end{quote}
Equivalently, $\neg\mathrm{Nonempty}(\cdots)$ as in
\lean{noCompositeRefinement}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose $R$ is such a refinement with state map $\mu$.
Theorem~\ref{thm:sync} gives a $C\parallel_K D$ step on a hidden
product label, so the hidden-step rule yields
$\mu((),\false)=\mu((),\true)$.
Observation preservation for $R$, together with congruence of
$(A\parallel_K D).\mathit{observe}$, implies
\[
(C\parallel_K D).\mathit{observe}\,((),\false)
=
(C\parallel_K D).\mathit{observe}\,((),\true).
\]
Projecting the second component contradicts Lemma~\ref{lem:obs}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Invalid-Congruence fails]
\label{cor:invalid}
The rule ``$C\preceqhm A$ implies
$C\parallel_K D\preceqhm A\parallel_K D$'' is false for
Definitions~\ref{def:weak}--\ref{def:par} and
Example~\ref{def:A}--\ref{def:KD}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}[The visible reading also fails]
\label{rem:visible}
If $\sync(\ping,\receive)$ is instead treated as visible, the abstract
product still has no matching step: $A$ admits no left-component step
with which to synchronize. The mechanized theorem discharges the
hidden reading used by ``synchronization is silent.'' The visible
reading is immediate from $A$'s empty step relation; it is not yet
packaged as a separately named checked Lean theorem.
\end{remark}

\section{Diagnosis}
\label{sec:diagnosis}
%------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Definition~\ref{def:weak}'s hidden-step clause constrains only the
component state map $\mu$. Definition~\ref{def:par} allows the same
concrete label to appear in a $\sync$ transition with a peer. Nothing
in the local rule prevents a hidden action from changing a peer
observation.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Local state-map equality is not contextual silence.}
\end{quote}

A tempting hygiene clause is that a hidden synchronization should map
to a composite silent observation. That clause does not repair
Example~\ref{def:A}--\ref{def:KD}: even if the product label is
classified hidden, $D$'s observation still changes from $\false$ to
$\true$, and Theorem~\ref{thm:no-composite} applies. The clause is
withdrawn.

The failure is not that hiding is meaningless. It is that hiding was
allowed to coexist with a live connection. The first repaired
obligation is therefore:

\begin{definition}[No hidden boundary]
\label{def:no-hidden-boundary}
If $h(\ell)=\true$ under the refinement witness, then $\ell$ is not
linked by the concrete connection $K_C$ to any peer label
(\lean{NoHiddenBoundary}).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Independence of no-hidden-boundary]
\label{lem:necessity-boundary}
\sloppy\lean{NoHiddenBoundary} fails for \lean{senderRefinement} and
\lean{connection}
(\lean{pingReceive_violates_noHiddenBoundary}).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\lean{senderHidden} hides $\ping$, and \lean{connection} links
$\ping$ to $\receive$, so $\ping$ is connected.
\end{proof}

%------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Conditional one-step observation-safety congruence}
\label{sec:conditional}
%------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Forming a product and lifting a refinement through a product are
different judgments. The latter needs an explicit interface.

Write $H(c,d)=(\mu(c),d)$ for the left-lift of a state map $\mu$
across a fixed peer (\lean{liftMap}). The default composite hiding
map \lean{compositeHiddenOf} classifies a left or synchronized
product label as hidden exactly when the underlying left label is
hidden, and treats peer-only steps as visible. The default composite
label map \lean{compositeMapOf} applies $m$ on the left component and
the identity on the peer. These are exactly the classifiers in
\eqref{eq:candidate}.

\begin{definition}[Wiring coverage]
\label{def:wiring}
A pair of connections $K_C,K_A$ is \emph{covered} by maps
$m$ and $\mathrm{id}$ (\lean{WiringCovered}) when every concrete
link maps to an abstract link, and every abstract link is the image
of some concrete link.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Isolation reflection]
\label{def:isolation}
The maps \emph{reflect isolation} (\lean{IsolationReflects}) when
every unconnected concrete left label remains unconnected after $m$:
if $\ell$ has no $K_C$-partner, then $m(\ell)$ has no $K_A$-partner.
\end{definition}

Coverage is a statement about the \emph{wires}. Isolation is a
statement about the \emph{free names}. They coincide when $m$ is
injective (\lean{isolation_of_wiring_injective}); they come apart
when $m$ collapses an independent concrete label onto a connected
abstract label.

\begin{definition}[Interface compatibility]
\label{def:iface}
A witness $R : C \preceqhm_{h,m} A$ is \emph{interface-compatible}
with peer $D$ and wirings $K_C,K_A$
(\lean{InterfaceCompatible}) when
Definition~\ref{def:no-hidden-boundary}, Definition~\ref{def:wiring},
and Definition~\ref{def:isolation} all hold.
\end{definition}

Observation of a product is componentwise by Definition~\ref{def:par},
so the usual observation-commutation premise is not an extra
hypothesis in this fragment. Synchronization of labels commutes with
$m$ because the default composite map applies $m$ on the left and
$\mathrm{id}$ on the right.

\begin{theorem}[Small-model one-step observation-safety lift]
\label{thm:weak-safety}
In the model of Definitions~\ref{def:weak}--\ref{def:par}, if
$R$ is interface-compatible with $D$, $K_C$, and $K_A$ in the sense
of Definition~\ref{def:iface}, then $H$ is a \lean{WeakRefines} of
the products under \lean{compositeHiddenOf} and \lean{compositeMapOf}
(\lean{weakConditionalCongruence_safety}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The Lean proof is a case analysis on product labels.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Init / observe.} Component initiality and $R$'s
  init and observe rules lift pairwise.
  \item \textbf{Hidden left.} Definition~\ref{def:no-hidden-boundary}
  makes the label unconnected, so the product permits a left-only
  step. $R$'s hidden-step rule gives $H(c,d)=H(c',d)$ with the peer
  fixed. A hidden $\sync$ is impossible: a connected label cannot be
  hidden.
  \item \textbf{Visible independent left.} $R$'s visible-step rule
  supplies an abstract left step.
  Definition~\ref{def:isolation} keeps the image unconnected on
  $K_A$, so the abstract product also permits the left-only step.
  \item \textbf{Independent peer.} Reuse the same $D$ step. Wiring
  reflection sends an abstract peer-link to a concrete one, so
  independence is preserved, and the left coordinate of $H$ is
  unchanged.
  \item \textbf{Synchronization.} Definition~\ref{def:no-hidden-boundary}
  forces the concrete left label visible. $R$'s visible-step rule
  and wiring coverage supply a matching abstract $\sync$ with the
  same peer transition.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

The theorem is an observation-safety result about one-step refinement
mappings on this LTS fragment. It does not transport liveness,
divergence, capabilities, or grades. It does not close full RFC~0008
conditional congruence.

\begin{corollary}[Observation-trace inclusion]
\label{cor:obs-trace}
Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:weak-safety}, every finite
sequence of product observations produced by a run of
$C\parallel_{K_C} D$ from an initial state is also the observation
sequence of the $H$-image run of $A\parallel_{K_A} D$. Equivalently,
one-step observation safety lifts to finite observation-trace
inclusion along the refinement map.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
By induction on run length: initialization and the observe clause of
$\lean{WeakRefines}$ give the base, and each product step preserves
observations by Theorem~\ref{thm:weak-safety}'s lifted witness.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Positive and negative controls}

\begin{lemma}[Visible ping/receive]
\label{lem:visible-sync}
The same connection as Example~\ref{def:A}--\ref{def:KD}, with $\ping$
classified visible and with an abstract sender that can take $\ping$,
admits a product one-step refinement
(\lean{VisibleSync.visibleSync_productRefinement}).
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Empty wiring and hidden $\tau$]
\label{lem:empty-tau}
A hidden left $\tau$ over the empty connection admits a product
one-step refinement
(\lean{EmptyWiringTau.emptyWiring_productRefinement}).
\end{lemma}

Lemma~\ref{lem:visible-sync} is the ping/receive system with the
offending hiding bit flipped: the wiring is unchanged, the local
refinement is now a strong one, and the lift goes through.
Lemma~\ref{lem:empty-tau} is the complementary case in which hiding
is genuine because there is nothing to connect.

\begin{lemma}[Isolation reflection is indispensable for the default lift]
\label{lem:isolation-necessary}
There exist systems and wirings such that
Definition~\ref{def:no-hidden-boundary} and
Definition~\ref{def:wiring} hold,
Definition~\ref{def:isolation} fails, and no \lean{WeakRefines} of
the products exists under the default composite classifiers
(\mbox{}\linebreak[1]\lean{CollidedAbstractWire.isolation_necessary_for_default_lift}).
\end{lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Wiring coverage is indispensable for the default lift]
\label{lem:wiring-necessary}
There exist systems and wirings such that
Definition~\ref{def:no-hidden-boundary} and
Definition~\ref{def:isolation} hold,
Definition~\ref{def:wiring} fails (an abstract wire on a label outside
the image of $m$), and no \lean{WeakRefines} of the products exists
under the default classifiers
(\mbox{}\linebreak[1]\lean{ExtraAbstractWire.wiring_necessary_for_default_lift};
axioms: none).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The concrete left component has labels $\tau$ and $\ping$; the
abstract left component has a single label $\mathsf{abs}$; both
concrete labels map to $\mathsf{abs}$. Only $\ping$ is wired to a
peer $\receive$, so $\tau$ is independent. Nothing is hidden, so
Definition~\ref{def:no-hidden-boundary} is vacuous. Wiring coverage
holds because the unique abstract wire reflects to $\ping$. Isolation
fails at $\tau$: $m(\tau)=\mathsf{abs}$ is connected. The concrete
product can take a left-only $\tau$ step; the abstract product cannot
take a left-only $\mathsf{abs}$ step. The default visible-left
obligation is therefore unsatisfiable.
\end{proof}

Together, Theorem~\ref{thm:no-composite} and
Lemmas~\ref{lem:necessity-boundary}, \ref{lem:isolation-necessary},
and~\ref{lem:wiring-necessary} say that each named premise of
Theorem~\ref{thm:weak-safety} is sharp for the default lift in the
hypothesis-independence sense: omitting that premise alone, while
keeping the others (or the ping/receive pattern for NHB), admits a
counterexample under \lean{compositeHiddenOf}/\lean{compositeMapOf}.
We do not claim necessity in any stronger, model-universal sense.

\begin{conjecture}[Residual weak congruence]
\label{conj:weak}
Theorem~\ref{thm:weak-safety} does not yet give congruence for

Definition~\ref{def:isolation} from Definition~\ref{def:wiring}

Lemma~\ref{lem:isolation-necessary} shows. Closing residual C1 / full
RFC~0008 CONDITIONAL-CONGRUENCE remains open.
\end{conjecture}

\section{An exact-action sibling}
\label{sec:strong}
%------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The repository also contains a \emph{different} refinement model:
exact-action (strong) refinement of open systems with explicit ports,
assumptions and guarantees, and whole-wiring equivalence
(\lean{NMLT.Behavior.OpenComposition}). Internal actions in that
model are ineligible for synchronization by construction, so the
ping/receive pattern cannot be expressed as a hidden connected
action. The results of this section are therefore not residual
obligations of Conjecture~\ref{conj:weak}; they live in another
formalism.

\begin{theorem}[Exact-action product congruence]
\label{thm:strong-lift}
In the OpenComposition model, with whole-wiring equivalence and the
stated composability premises,
\lean{StrongRefinement.compositionCongruence} holds: left-component
refinement lifts through parallel composition, and product
receptiveness and composability are preserved as stated in the
development. (Self-contained reading: exact matching of actions, no
weak hiding classifier, ports rather than free labels, and internal
actions barred from synchronization.)
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:strong-lift} is not a proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:weak-safety} or of Conjecture~\ref{conj:weak}, and it
is not residual C1. By the module's own statement it claims nothing
about weak hiding, label maps beyond the strong model, capability
partition, grades, or liveness. We include it as evidence that an
interface-aware positive lifting theorem is available once hiding
through connections is ruled out structurally.

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:rw}
%------------------------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{Refinement mappings and stuttering.}
Abadi and Lamport's refinement mappings~\cite{abadiLamport1991} and
Lamport's TLA~\cite{lamport2002} treat implementation steps as
abstract stuttering under a state map. Definition~\ref{def:weak}'s
hidden-step rule is in that spirit: hidden steps preserve abstract
\emph{state}. The counterexample shows that port synchronization is
an additional obligation those local rules do not discharge. A
stuttering step that is still connected is not a stutter of the
composite.

\paragraph{Weak equivalences and congruence formats.}
Classical weak bisimulation and weak simulation
\cite{milner1989,vanGlabbeek1993} close silent transitions under
$\tau^*$ and ask for matching after silent sequences. Congruence
formats for weak equivalences (e.g.\ the tyft/tyxt and ntyft/ntyxt
tradition, and later rule formats for weak bisimulation)
\cite{grooteVaandrager1992,ulidowskiPhillips2002} study syntactic
conditions under which a weak equivalence is a congruence for a
process language's operators. Our contribution is complementary and
narrower: we fix an \emph{already chosen} one-step refinement mapping
(no $\tau^*$), fix a synchronized product, and mechanize the failure
of unconditional contextual lift when hiding coexists with a live
connection---together with hypothesis-independence controls for a
conditional repair. We do not propose a new congruence format.

\paragraph{I/O automata and simulations.}
I/O automata distinguish input, output, and internal actions and
prove composition theorems under compatibility and
receptiveness~\cite{lynchTuttle1987,lynchVaandrager1995}. Forward
simulations in that tradition are not expected to lift through
composition without those premises. Our result is consistent with
that lesson: the failure appears when compatibility-style premises
are omitted from a TLA-style hiding rule. We do not propose a new
automaton model.

\paragraph{Interface automata.}
de~Alfaro and Henzinger's interface automata~\cite{deAlfaroHenzinger2001}
make composition a partial operation, failing when an output is not
accepted. The moral is again that composition is not automatic.
Theorem~\ref{thm:weak-safety} takes the complementary route of
stating the missing premises rather than making the product partial.

\paragraph{Process calculus hiding.}
CCS restriction and hiding~\cite{milner1989}, and CSP
concealment~\cite{hoare1985}, remove names from the interface. The
mistake in \eqref{eq:invalid} is to hide a name that is still in the
interface of the product. Once $\ping$ is connected it is not an
internal name of $C\parallel_K D$, regardless of $C$'s local
classification.

\paragraph{Stutter-invariant temporal logic.}
Peled and Wilke~\cite{peledWilke} characterize stutter-invariant LTL
by the absence of $\mathsf{next}$. That motivates separating
action-level claims from observation transport. It does not, by
itself, police connected hidden labels.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conc}
%------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A hidden action is not contextually silent if it is still connected.
We mechanized the resulting failure of unconditional one-step refinement
congruence under synchronization, and we mechanized a matching
conditional observation-safety lift on the same small model: no hidden
connected labels, covered wirings, and a label map that preserves
independence. The exact-action theorem shows that a stronger model
can rule the failure out by construction; it is a different model, not
residual C1. What remains is to carry the one-step theorem up through
resources, grades, and fairness, and to connect these transition systems
to the surface language, without weakening the interface conditions that
the counterexamples show are independently indispensable for the default
lift.
